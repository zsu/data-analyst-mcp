# Estimate patterns for IRS SOI tables

Use this file when a question asks what a tax change would raise or cost, who pays a tax, how large a tax base
is, or how IRS collections are made up. The published tables give the base and the tax at today's law; an
estimate built on them is **static**: it holds incomes and behavior fixed. Say so in the answer, every time.

The method each recipe follows:
1. Fetch the table (`table`), long by default; keep only the rows and columns the estimate needs (`rows`,
   `columns`, `section`).
2. Compute with `compute` on the fetched CSV (`filter`, `derive`, `groupby` + `rollup`, `pivot_wider`, `deflate`);
   every number in the answer comes from a fetched table or a step recorded in the lineage.
3. State the year type (tax, fiscal, filing), the units (`UNITS`: usually thousands of dollars), the flags of
   the cells used (`FLAG`), and that the estimate is static.

Never invent a parameter. A behavioral response (an elasticity of taxable income, a realization response for
capital gains, avoidance) is an assumption: use it only when the user gives one or a cited source does, record it
with `record_decisions`, and show the static number beside the adjusted one.

## Individual income tax

- **Revenue from a change in a bracket rate** (`in_3.5`, tax generated by rate). Column "Tax generated at
  specified rate | <r> percent | Income taxed at rate" is the income taxed at that rate. A change of d
  percentage points raises about income taxed at the rate x d / 100. Check: tax generated at the rate / income
  taxed at the rate = the rate. Example: TY2023, 37 percent: 1,159,707,580 thousand dollars of income, 429,091,805
  thousand of tax; one more point = about $11.6 billion a year, static, before credits interact. A change in a
  bracket threshold needs the income near the threshold, which the table does not give: say so.
  ```json
  {"name": "rate37", "endpoint": "table", "params": {"table": "in_3.5", "year": 2023, "rows": ["Total"], "columns": ["37 percent"]}}
  ```
  `compute` steps: `{"filter": "COLUMN == \"Tax generated at specified rate | 37 percent | Income taxed at rate\""}`,
  `{"derive": {"change_thousand_dollars": "VALUE * 0.01"}}`.
- **Average and effective tax rates by income** (`in_1.1`, `in_3.3`): total income tax / AGI by size of AGI,
  from the same table and year. Table 1.1 has the percentages already in "Taxable returns | Total income tax |
  Percent of | Adjusted gross income less deficit".
- **Who pays: shares of the top 1 percent and others** (`shares_4.1`, every year 2001 onward in one file):
  sections "Adjusted gross income share", "Total income tax share", "Average tax rate", "Adjusted gross income
  floor on percentiles" (current and constant dollars). Quote the share with its year and the percentile
  definition (returns excluding dependents, positive AGI).
- **Credits and deductions** (`in_2.5` EITC, `in_2.1` itemized deductions, `in_1.4` sources of income and
  adjustments): the cost of a credit change is about the change per claim x the number of claims in the affected
  AGI classes, static. Itemized deductions are in Table 2.1 by type (SALT, mortgage interest, charitable).
- **Capital gains** (`in_1.4A`): gains and losses by size of AGI; rate changes on gains are where a realization
  response matters most: never leave it out without saying so.
- **Over time**: one workbook per year (`start_year`/`end_year`); compare `COLUMN` text across years (column
  numbers move); deflate with a price index fetched from bls or bea (`deflate`), and say which.

## Business taxes

- **Corporate tax base and effective rate** (`corp_2.1`, `corp_1`, `corp_5.1`; C corporations alone:
  `corp_2.3`, `corp_3.3`, `corp_5.3`): "Net income (less deficit)", "Income subject to tax", "Total income tax
  before credits", "Total income tax after credits". Average effective rate = tax after credits / net income (or
  / income subject to tax: name the denominator). Static revenue of a statutory rate change on C corporations
  = income subject to tax x d / 100, before credits and the minimum tax; label it an upper bound.
- **By industry or size**: `corp_1` / `corp_5.x` rows are industries (`LEVEL` and `PARENT` give the hierarchy:
  sum one level only); `corp_2.x` columns are asset-size classes. Cells marked `*` have high sampling
  variability (the CVs are in `corp_1-CV`); `d` cells are deleted but counted in the totals.
- **Pass-through income** (`partnership_1`, `corp_2.4` / `corp_6.1` for S corporations, `soleprop_1`): net income
  by industry. A change aimed at pass-through income (the qualified business income deduction, a surtax) starts
  from these totals; the share that reaches individuals in each bracket is not in these tables (only microdata).
- **Corporate profits in the national accounts** differ from SOI net income (`historical_8`, `historical_15`
  reconcile them); use bea for NIPA profits.

## Collections and administration (IRS Data Book, fiscal years)

- **Collections by type of tax** (`databook_1-6`: gross collections 1960 onward in one file; `databook_1-1`: gross,
  refunds and net for the last two years) and **by state** (`databook_1-5`, `databook_1-8` refunds). Gross
  collections are cash received in the fiscal year, of any tax year; state is the filer's address (a business
  pays where its headquarters files), not where income was earned: say so for state comparisons.
- **Excise taxes by type** (`historical_20`, fiscal years 1999 onward).
- **Enforcement**: audit coverage and recommended additional tax (`databook_3-1`); cost of collecting $100
  (`databook_6-2`).

## Estate and gift tax, wealth

- **Exemption or rate change** (`estate_1`, by size of gross estate and tax status): the number of taxable
  returns, the taxable estate, the tentative tax and the net estate tax by size class. Raising the exemption
  removes the returns below it and lowers the rest; the table gives the classes, not the returns: say how a class
  was split, or keep to class boundaries. Filing years mix years of death.
- **By state** (`estate_2`), **charitable bequests** (`estate_3`), **gifts** (`gift_1`, whole dollars, not
  thousands), **top wealth holders** (`wealth_1` ... `_6`, estimates from estate returns, millions of dollars).

## Nonprofits

- **Sector size** (`eo_1`, `eo_3`, `pf_1`, `historical_16`): revenue, expenses and assets by asset size or Code
  section, from Form 990 samples. **One organization or a state** (`eo_bmf` registry; `eo_extract` Form 990 data
  by EIN or state): the registry's amounts come from each organization's latest return, of different years.
- The charitable deduction's cost is an individual-income-tax item (`in_2.1`, charitable contributions by size
  of AGI), not a nonprofit table.

## What to write

Lead with the number and its frame: "One more percentage point on the 37 percent rate would have raised about
$11.6 billion on tax year 2023 income, before any change in behavior (IRS SOI, Publication 1304 Table 3.5)."
Then the base, the year type, the units, the flags, and what the static estimate leaves out. Ship the fetched
tables and the computed exhibit; the citation names the table and year.
